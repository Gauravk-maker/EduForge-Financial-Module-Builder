# Content Creator Guide

Welcome to EduForge Studio. This guide helps you build a financial learning lesson without writing code.

## Getting started
1. Open the builder in a modern browser.
2. Select the lesson title at the top and type a clear, learner-friendly name.
3. Read the short description below the title and keep it focused on what a learner will understand.
4. Select the plus (+) button in the left panel or the “Add another block” control.
5. Choose a building block from the list. You can search for words such as “quiz”, “image” or “calculator”.
6. Select a block on the canvas to open its settings panel.
7. Type your content in the fields. Changes appear in the learner preview automatically.
8. Use the desktop, tablet and mobile controls above the preview to check the lesson on different screens.
9. Select Export module when the validation message is clear. Your lesson downloads as a portable file.

## Overview of the 15 building blocks

### 1. Rich text
Use this for explanations, introductions and lesson summaries. For example, explain the difference between needs and wants before asking learners to make a budget. Keep paragraphs short and use headings to make the lesson easy to scan.

### 2. Image
Use a relevant image to make a concept easier to understand, such as a labelled monthly budget chart. Add an image URL and meaningful alternative text describing what the image communicates. A caption can provide context. Alternative text is important for learners using screen readers.

### 3. Video embed
Use a YouTube or Vimeo lesson to demonstrate a topic such as how interest accumulates. Paste the video link. Check that the link is correct and that the video is suitable for your learners.

### 4. Multiple-choice quiz
Use this block to check understanding after an explanation. Enter a question, provide two to six answer options, and mark at least one correct option. Add explanations so learners understand why an answer is right or wrong. The settings panel lets you set points, attempts and a timer.

### 5. True/false quiz
Use a true-or-false question for a quick knowledge check. For example: “An emergency fund can help cover unexpected expenses.” Choose the correct answer and explain it in plain language.

### 6. EMI calculator
Use this when teaching home loans, vehicle loans or other monthly repayments. Learners can explore how the loan amount, interest rate and repayment period affect the monthly instalment. The calculator shows the estimated monthly payment, total interest, total payment and a month-by-month repayment schedule.

### 7. SIP calculator
Use this when introducing regular investing. Set the monthly contribution, expected annual return and number of years. Learners can compare the amount they contribute with the estimated future value. The result is an illustration, not a promise of investment returns.

### 8. Compound interest calculator
Use this to show how money may grow when returns are added back to the balance. Set the starting amount, annual rate, duration and compounding frequency. The chart helps learners compare growth over time.

### 9. Callout
Use a callout to make an important point stand out. Choose a tip, information, warning, important or caution style. For example, remind learners that credit card bills should be paid on time.

### 10. Divider
Use a divider to create a visual pause between lesson sections. Choose a solid, dashed, dotted or gradient line.

### 11. Accordion / FAQ
Use this for supporting details that learners can open when needed. Add questions such as “What is a credit score?” and provide a short answer under each one.

### 12. Progress tracker
Use this to show the learner where they are in a sequence. Add the total number of steps, choose the current step and provide labels such as Learn, Explore, Practice, Review and Complete.

### 13. Achievement badge
Use a badge to celebrate a learning milestone. Give it a title and description, and explain the condition required to unlock it. For example, award a “Budgeting Basics” badge after a learner completes a budgeting lesson.

### 14. Code snippet
Use this when a lesson benefits from showing a small structured example, such as a simple JSON representation of a savings goal. Choose the language and enter the example.

### 15. Concept explainer
Use this to break a difficult idea into a sequence of steps. For example, explain compound growth by showing the starting amount, the return earned and how future returns can build on earlier returns.

## Using the calculators

### EMI calculator
Enter the loan principal, annual interest rate and repayment period. The principal must be between ₹10,000 and ₹10 crore, the annual rate between 1% and 36%, and the tenure between 1 and 360 months. The monthly payment uses a reducing-balance calculation. Total interest is the sum of payments minus the original principal. Use the amortisation schedule to inspect each month's opening balance, payment, principal portion, interest portion and closing balance.

### SIP calculator
Enter the monthly investment (₹500 to ₹10,00,000), expected annual return (1% to 30%) and duration (1 to 40 years). The estimate assumes contributions at the beginning of each month. The future value is an illustration based on the return assumption and does not guarantee market performance.

### Compound interest calculator
Enter a positive starting amount, annual interest rate and duration. Choose annual, half-yearly, quarterly or monthly compounding. The growth chart shows the estimated value at each year-end.

## Creating quizzes
Write one clear question at a time. Add two to six options for a multiple-choice question. Mark the correct answer using the checkbox. Use the settings panel to choose points, the attempt limit and an optional timer. In the learner preview, choose an answer and submit it to see feedback. For a true/false question, select which statement is correct and provide an explanation.

## Arranging your lesson
Drag the grip handle at the top-left of a block to move it. You can also select a block and use Alt + Up or Alt + Down. Select the copy icon to duplicate a block. Select the bin icon to remove a block; confirm the action first. If you make a mistake, use Undo. Redo brings back a change you have undone. The history panel shows recent changes.

## Previewing your lesson
The preview panel shows the learner-facing lesson. Select the desktop, tablet or mobile control to change the preview width. Check that text is readable, calculators are usable and tables do not overflow. The preview updates when you edit the lesson. Use the theme control to check the alternate preview appearance.

## Exporting and importing
Select Export module to download a portable lesson file. The builder checks required fields before export. To continue editing a saved lesson, select Import and choose its JSON file. Import replaces the current lesson after you confirm that unsaved changes can be discarded. Imported blocks receive fresh internal identifiers.

## Troubleshooting
- **I accidentally deleted a block.** Select Undo straight away. The previous lesson version is restored.
- **My block is not editable.** It may be locked. Select the lock control to unlock it, then edit again.
- **My block does not appear in the preview.** Check its visibility control. Hidden blocks remain in the editor but are not shown to learners.
- **The export does not start.** Review the validation message. Check that the lesson has a title and at least one block, and that quizzes have enough options and a correct answer.
- **My image is missing.** Check the image link and add alternative text. The link must be reachable by the learner's browser.
- **I imported the wrong lesson.** Use Undo if the import has just happened, or import the correct saved file again.
- **The calculator does not accept a value.** Check the permitted range shown in the settings. Enter a valid amount, rate and duration.

## Glossary
- **Block:** One piece of lesson content, such as a paragraph, quiz or calculator.
- **Module:** The complete lesson made from multiple blocks.
- **Preview:** A view of what learners will see.
- **Export:** Save a copy of the lesson to a file.
- **Import:** Bring a saved lesson file into the builder.
- **Undo:** Reverse your most recent change.
- **Redo:** Restore a change you have undone.
- **Quiz:** A question that checks what a learner understands.
- **Calculator:** An interactive tool that estimates a financial result from entered values.
- **Alternative text:** A written description of an image for learners who cannot see it.
